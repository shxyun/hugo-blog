A few days ago the talk was pensions. Now a new variation has surfaced: a caller claiming to be from the human resources bureau offers to upgrade your social security card to a "gold card," pulls you into a group chat, and promises a monthly subsidy paid onto the card.

It sounds tempting. It is also false. Beijing's Human Resources and Social Security Bureau has responded directly: the "gold card" and the "monthly subsidy" are a scam.

## 1. What is fake about it: three official statements

Beijing Daily's app reported on October 10 that people have been impersonating Beijing bureau staff, using the promise of a "gold social security card" to pull residents into group chats, with monthly subsidies offered as bait. The bureau's response was blunt: this is fraud.

The official position is short. Three points cover it:

- There is only one version of the social security card, and it is blue. There is no "gold card."
- Human resources and social security departments do not set up group chats or recruit members on the pretext of "receiving social security benefits."
- Treat messages of this kind from unknown sources with suspicion, and protect your personal information.

In plain terms: any call or text telling you to "upgrade to a gold card" or "join a group for subsidies" can be hung up on or deleted.

## 2. Officials have debunked this "subsidy" angle before

Scams that wave the word "subsidy" change outfits every few months and come back.

In April this year the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security warned through CCTV about messages urging people to click a link for a "2026 comprehensive subsidy." Don't believe it, don't click. Then in July, the Dalian human resources bureau issued its own alert: anything pitched as an "inside channel" or "agent service" that quietly asks for a "processing fee" is a scam.

The label changes every year. The machinery does not: get you to believe, get you to click, get you to pay.

## 3. When in doubt, verify through official channels

Rather than studying the scammer's latest trick, remember one line: social security business and benefit payments go through official counters and official platforms. Not group chats, not links, and never a fee collected up front.

If a message leaves you unsure, verify it or ask for help through official channels:

- Call **12333**, the national human resources and social security hotline. Policy questions and personal benefit questions both go there.
- Check **official platforms**: your local bureau's government website and its official WeChat account.
- If you have already been defrauded or transferred money, call **110** or the anti-fraud line **96110** immediately, and install the National Anti-Fraud Center app if needed.

One more basic rule: anyone asking for your verification code, bank card number or password is a fraudster, whoever they claim to be.

## 4. A word for the family

Older relatives are the most likely to take messages like this at face value. For adult children, one sentence—"don't trust this kind of message, call 12333 and ask"—usually works better than a long explanation.

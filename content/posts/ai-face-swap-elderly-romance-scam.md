Suppose a "very well-off" man starts sending warm messages to your aunt through a screen, promising to spend his later years with her. Hold the congratulations. That face may not belong to a real person.

This is not alarmism. CCTV has reported on it, the criminal investigation bureau of the Ministry of Public Security has issued warnings, and the Ministry of Civil Affairs has put out advisories. What follows does not teach the method. It covers how to recognize the pattern and how to defend against it, with a checklist for adult children at the end.

## 1. Three features that give these "late-life romances" away

The tricks keep changing. Three features stay constant.

- **The warmth comes too fast.** A friend request is barely accepted before the questions about her health begin, and the "conditions are so good it feels rude to say no."
- **The persona is too perfect.** Photos and videos may be AI-generated. The same face, under a different name, can turn up on several women's phones.
- **It always ends at money.** First a "matching fee" or a "deposit," and once trust is built, "help me tide things over" or "let me show you an investment."

One line is worth memorizing: the meeting is always postponed, and the conversation always circles back to a transfer. Stop there.

## 2. What the authorities actually said

**Ministry of Public Security criminal investigation bureau, warning dated May 19, 2026:** anyone who opens with a military identity, moves quickly to a romantic relationship and then steers toward investment is, without exception, a fraudster. Real service members do not look for marriage partners online, and they certainly do not offer "inside investments with guaranteed returns."

**State Council Information Office briefing, July 24, 2026:** the National Anti-Fraud Center app now includes AI content detection. You can upload a suspicious image, video or voice clip and have it checked for AI traces. One caveat: the tool only answers whether something was made by AI. It does not verify whether an ID document is genuine, so don't let anyone turn it into "proof" that a person is real.

**Ministry of Civil Affairs, advisory dated September 22, 2026:** watch for fake "elderly care apps" posing as official services, forged documents used by people claiming to be staff distributing subsidies, and any scheme demanding an "activation fee" up front. The ministry has never run anything called "Minhuitong."

## 3. Real cases—read them as individual cases

In August 2026, CCTV's legal affairs program reported on a type of AI matchmaking fraud. Dating agencies were targeting single middle-aged and older women, and every "male candidate" photo was AI-generated. The same image appeared under different names to different clients, and contact ended once the service fee was paid.

There is also a case reported by Chengdu High-tech Police and covered by Sichuan Legal Daily and Chuanguan News. A ring took in more than 1 million yuan in under a year, with about 50 victims, all single women over 45. Arrests were made in Sichuan and Yunnan in early July 2026, and 28 people were placed under criminal coercive measures.

To be clear: this is one case, not a national statistic. The playbook, though, is stable, and knowing the playbook helps more than memorizing numbers.

## 4. A checklist for adult children

Rather than repeating warnings, do these four things for your parents.

1. **Any money question gets a real phone call first.** Video can be swapped, voices can be cloned. Before any transfer, call back a number you already have saved and confirm it is the person.
2. **Install the National Anti-Fraud Center app on their phones** and teach them the AI content detection feature: upload the picture, video or voice clip that seems off and check it. Remember, it detects AI traces, which is not the same as proving someone's identity.
3. **Save 96110 to speed dial.** This is the anti-fraud warning line. If it calls, it usually means you or a family member is being targeted, so answer it. If money has already gone out, call 110 right away.
4. **Run a friend-list check.** Go through the contacts your parents added recently and can't explain, and delete the suspicious ones. Ten minutes beforehand beats repair work afterward.

## Closing

Love is not something to block. But warmth from a screen should survive one real phone call first. Forward this checklist to the family group chat. Every extra reader is one fewer victim.
